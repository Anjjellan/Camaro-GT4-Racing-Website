Track History: Circuit Zolder

Circuit Overview

Zolder, Belgium's first purpose-built racetrack, may not rival Spa-Francorchamps in fame but has its own rich history. While Formula One no longer races here, Zolder has hosted high-profile series such as DTM (most recently in 2019) and the FIA WTCR (2020). A busy schedule of national events, along with 24-hour GT and touring car races, keeps Zolder active.

Track Features

  • Length: 4 km
  • Turns: 10, including 3 chicanes
  • Key Features: Sacramentsheuvel, long straights, tight corners
  • Notable Events: Former host of Formula 1 Grand Prix, current GT3/GT4 racing series

Circuit History

Racing in the Limburg area dates to the 1950s. Zolder’s motor club initially hosted street races but sought a safer, permanent track. They established the Omloop van Terlaemen circuit in 1961, with local landowner Antoine Palmers de Terlaemen’s support. In 1962, Dutch designer John Hugenholtz improved the track’s layout, and it opened in 1963 with Rob Slotemaker winning the first race. Zolder soon became a fixture on Belgium's racing calendar.

Formula One Era and Tragedy

With Spa facing challenges, Zolder and Nivelles-Baulers shared the Belgian Grand Prix from 1972. Zolder’s F1 journey, however, included difficulties—a tragic 1981 pit-lane accident and, in 1982, the fatal crash of Gilles Villeneuve.

Updates and Legacy

After Formula One’s permanent move to Spa in 1983, Zolder focused on touring car events, including DTM. Several updates improved track safety, while changes in 2001 realigned the canal loop. In recent years, international events like Superleague Formula and DTM have returned to Zolder, keeping its legacy alive.
